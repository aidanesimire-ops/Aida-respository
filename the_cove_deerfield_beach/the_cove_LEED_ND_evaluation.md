# The Cove (Deerfield Beach, FL) — LEED v4 for Neighborhood Development Evaluation

**Subject:** The Cove neighborhood boundary (the mapped area) assessed against the
**LEED v4 for Neighborhood Development (LEED-ND)** checklist (110 pts; Certified 40–49,
Silver 50–59, Gold 60–79, Platinum 80+).
**Date:** 2026-07-03 · **Assessment type:** Interpretive / planning-discussion (not an official certification)

---

## ⚠️ Read this first — what this evaluation is and isn't

**LEED-ND rates *new* development and redevelopment *projects*** — it is scored during
planning/construction and depends heavily on building certifications, energy/water performance,
and construction practices. **The Cove is an existing, built-out single-family waterfront
neighborhood dating to 1956.** Running an existing neighborhood through LEED-ND is therefore an
*interpretive* exercise: useful to expose location/urban-form strengths and gaps for a planning
conversation, but **not** a path to an actual plaque. Scores below estimate *how the existing
neighborhood aligns with each credit's intent*, not points a certifying body would award.

## Headline verdict

| | Result |
|---|---|
| **Interpretive score** | **~20 "likely" points, up to ~38** if every partial/uncertain credit were pursued and documented. Realistic landing: **high-20s to low-30s.** |
| **Certification tier** | **Below Certified** (needs 40). Even the optimistic ceiling (38) falls short. |
| **Could it actually be certified as-is?** | **No.** It fails required **prerequisites** (see below), independent of the point total. |

**Why:** The Cove scores respectably on *where it is and what's around it* (infill location,
parks, a walkable retail node, schools, canopy) but poorly on *what LEED-ND most rewards* —
compact density, housing diversity, high-frequency transit, and green-building/energy performance.

---

## Certification reality: the prerequisites (decisive)

Prerequisites are pass/fail and required regardless of points. Several are **not met** or
**at risk** for an existing 1956 neighborhood:

| Category | Prerequisite | Status | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| GIB | Certified Green Building | ❌ **Not met** | Requires ≥1 building certified under a green rating system; none confirmed. |
| GIB | Minimum Building Energy Performance | ❌ **Not met** | 1956-era housing stock, no energy-performance program. |
| GIB | Indoor Water Use Reduction | ⚠️ **Unknown** | Standard existing fixtures; not documented. |
| SLL | Wetland & Water Body Conservation | ⚠️ **At risk** | Built to seawalls on man-made finger canals; no vegetated buffers. |
| SLL | Floodplain Avoidance | ⚠️ **At risk** | Largely **FEMA Zone AE** (1%-annual-chance coastal flood); qualifies only via the previously-developed/infill path. |
| NPD | Compact Development | ⚠️ **At risk** | Single-family density (~3–5 DU/acre) is below LEED-ND compact-development targets. |
| NPD | Connected & Open Community | ⚠️ **At risk** | Western street grid is well-connected, but eastern finger-canal cul-de-sacs cut intersection density. |

➡️ **Bottom line:** the green-building/energy prerequisites alone make as-is certification
impossible without new construction or deep retrofits.

## Strengths (what The Cove already does well)
1. **Location efficiency** — genuine infill between two arterials and the Intracoastal, not sprawl.
2. **Parks & waterfront recreation** — Sullivan Park, the fishing pier, the beach, Deerfield Island Park.
3. **A real neighborhood center** — the Cove Shopping Center gives a walkable retail/dining node + farmers market.
4. **Schools, canopy, and an organized civic association** — the "soft" neighborhood fabric LEED-ND rewards.

## Weaknesses (what pulls the score down)
1. **Low residential density** — the single biggest structural gap (Compact Development prereq + credit).
2. **Limited housing diversity & affordability** — predominantly single-family; costly waterfront.
3. **Car-dependence / thin transit** — no adjacent high-frequency or rail transit.
4. **Coastal floodplain exposure** — FEMA Zone AE; a resilience flag as much as a scoring one.
5. **No green-building/energy program** — the decisive prerequisite gap for an existing neighborhood.

## What would actually move the needle (planning levers)
- **Along the US-1 / Hillsboro edges:** allow modest **mixed-use, higher-density infill** (this is where density/housing-diversity/transit points live — and where the neighborhood's own boundary logic already flags an "edge condition").
- **Transit:** partner with BCT for higher-frequency service / better shelters on Federal Hwy.
- **Green stormwater & heat:** canal-front green infrastructure, tree-canopy targets, cool/permeable paving at the shopping-center lot (Rainwater, Heat Island, RP credits).
- **Resilience:** treat Zone AE flood adaptation as a first-class planning objective (freeboard, seawall/living-shoreline standards).
- **Any new construction** in the district: require a green-building standard so the GIB prerequisites become reachable over time.
