01

Why this is a no-brainer at $1.1M

Four reasons, in order of force

The listing asks $1,600,000 for an 8.25% return with no rent growth and no corporate credit. That deal does not work. The same asset at $1,100,000 is a completely different investment — and the arithmetic is not close.

$0Paid for the upside

Capitalizing conventional market rent of $22/SF gives a value of $1,106,000. Our offer is $1,100,000. We are buying the building at its plain-vanilla, re-let-to-a-bank value — so the entire cannabis rent premium, the option, the drive-thru and the excess land cost us nothing.

78%Returned before expiry

Contracted rent through 31 January 2033 totals $858,000 — 78% of the purchase price back in hand before the lease even matures, with no reliance on renewal, refinancing or resale. If the tenant exercises the option, contracted rent reaches $1,628,000, or 148% of our basis.

68%Covered by dirt and shell

Land plus vacant building supports roughly $750,000 — 68% of what we are paying. Strip that out and we are paying about $350,000 for a $132,000 income stream. Two and a half years of rent covers the entire at-risk portion of the basis.

+6.2%The worst case still pays

Run the tenant out of business, leave it dark, and sell the land and shell — the deal still returns 6.2% unlevered. At the $1.6M ask that same scenario returns −1.4%. At our number the downside is not a loss; it is a lower win.

The whole thesis in one line. Every argument the seller makes for the price — the 8.25% cap, the drive-thru, the below-replacement-cost basis, the option, the 3.13 acres — is an argument we accept. We simply decline to pay for the one thing that is genuinely uncertain: whether a single-store cannabis operator is still there in 2033. At $1,100,000 we do not need to know.

06

Who backfills it, in order

Re-tenanting playbook

A vault, a 3-lane drive-thru and three acres on a 15,370-VPD highway is a specific and rentable instrument. There is a real queue for it.

1. Credit unions & community banks

The cleanest backfill available: the building already is one. Vault, drive-thru lanes, teller infrastructure and parking transfer at near-zero TI, so occupancy comes fastest and cheapest here. South Jersey credit unions continue to add suburban branches. $22–26/SF, 6–12 months, 8.8–10.4% on our basis.

2. Urgent care, dental, veterinary, dialysis

Deepest tenant pool in the market. The drive-thru bay converts to a covered patient drop-off, parking is oversized, and Route 30 frontage drives walk-in volume. These tenants sign 10–15 year NNN leases with escalations — which is exactly what makes a future exit financeable and re-rates the asset. $22–26/SF, 8.8–10.4%.

3. Another NJ cannabis licensee

Holds the full $30/SF. Waterford Township permits two retail cannabis licenses and the incumbent holds one — thin demand, but genuinely scarce supply, and a licensee needing a compliant site with a drive-thru has nowhere else to go on this corridor. Upside, not our base case.

4. Conventional retail & service

Liquor, convenience, pharmacy, auto parts, fitness, tax and check-cashing. Always available on this corridor at $18–22/SF, which still yields 7.2–8.8% on our basis. This is the floor, not the plan — but it is a floor that pays.

Separately, the ±2.5 acres of idle land is a distinct income opportunity in its own right — see section 07.

The land — 2.5 acres we are not paying for

Excluded from every return above

The building covers 3.2% of the site. Once you subtract parking and the drive-thru stacking lanes, roughly 2.5 acres sit idle on a highway corridor between Philadelphia and the shore. None of the returns in this analysis assume a dollar of it.

3.13 acres total, less ~0.10 for the building and ~0.55 for parking and drive-thru stacking. Zoning is PHB — Planned Highway Business, and Atco sits in the Pinelands Regional Growth Area, the most development-permissive of the six CMP designations.

A single outparcel ground lease plus an EV charging licence conservatively adds $75,000/yr of NOI on land that currently produces nothing — lifting yield on cost from 12.0% to 18.8%.

Capitalizing that income at 9.25% and netting out an estimated $150,000 of subdivision, entitlement and site work creates roughly $661,000 of value — on a $1.1M basis, that is a second deal hiding inside the first.

This is the real prize. With a pad in place, even the bad case — tenant gone, building re-let at only $18/SF — still yields 14.0% on cost. The land does not just add upside; it removes the downside.

On EV charging specifically — set expectations correctly. Tesla's own historic model pays site hosts little or no ground rent; the value to them is the location, and their newer "Supercharger for Business" program is host-owned, meaning we would fund the hardware. The rent is with third-party operators — EVgo, Electrify America, Applegreen and similar — who fund 100% of capex and pay either a fixed licence fee or 10–25% of gross charging revenue.

So: pursue a third-party charging operator on a revenue-share licence, and treat a Tesla installation as a traffic-and-visibility play rather than an income line.

What has to be true — verify before hard money

The gating item: does the lease demise the whole parcel?

The offering markets ±3.13 AC as the leased premises. If the lease demises the entire lot to the tenant, we cannot develop any of it during the term without their consent. Read the demised-premises clause first — before anything else on this page matters. Landlords commonly reserve development and subdivision rights, and where they have not, consent is usually purchasable for a modest rent credit. But confirm it, and do not pay for the land upside until you have.

Entitlement and utilities

Subdivision and site-plan approval run through the Waterford Township Planning Board with Pinelands Commission review — Regional Growth Area is the favourable designation, but review still applies. Critically, the CMP requires nonresidential parcels under one acre to be served by centralized wastewater: confirm sewer availability and capacity, because a septic-only site materially limits what a pad can be.

Parking and access

Any pad must leave the existing tenant with compliant parking and preserve drive-thru stacking and circulation. Run a parking count against the PHB standard before assuming a full acre is severable, and expect to grant reciprocal access easements.

Keep it out of the base case

Every return figure in this analysis excludes the land. That is deliberate. We are buying at a price justified by the building alone, so the land is free option value — and we should never bid it up. If it is later found to be encumbered by the lease or blocked at Pinelands, our basis is unaffected.

How we defend the number

Lead with the rent, not the cap rate

Do not argue about whether 8.25% is the right cap. Argue that $30.00/SF is not a market rent — and put the corridor comps in front of them. Every one of the seller's own value arguments survives; only the rent assumption changes.

Point at the assessor

Camden County's own equalized value is $1,271,000. A third party with no stake in the outcome already says this property is not worth $1.6M. That is the single hardest fact to argue with.

Name the financing reality

Most buyers cannot get a loan on cannabis collateral. An all-cash offer with a short close is worth real money to a seller whose alternative is a financed buyer who cannot perform. Sell the certainty.

Give them a ladder, not a wall

If $1.1M is rejected outright, $1,250,000 still delivers 9.3% expected and 13.1% on renewal. Hold there. Above $1.25M we are paying more than the property is worth — let it go, there will be another one.

Bottom line. At $1,100,000 we buy a corner drive-thru property on a 15,370-VPD highway at $250/SF — below replacement cost, below the tax assessor's own equalized value, and at the same price the building would fetch with an ordinary tenant paying ordinary rent. We collect a 12% coupon for six and a half years, recover 78% of our capital before the lease even expires, and hold four separate re-tenanting paths.

Then there is the land. Monetizing the idle 2.5 acres through a single outparcel ground lease and an EV charging licence conservatively adds $75,000 of NOI and roughly $661,000 of value — carried at zero in every return figure above. Execute it and yield on cost goes to 18.8%, and even a dark building re-let at $18/SF still pays 14.0%.

The expected return is 12.0% before any of that. The worst case is +6.2%. There is no version of this where we lose money — and that is the definition of a deal worth doing.
