1. The rent never grows

Zero bumps across ten years. At 2.5% inflation, the $30.00/SF signed in 2023 is worth about $23.20/SF in 2033 dollars. Your 8.25% coupon is also your total return — there is no growth component to sell into.

2. The rent is a cannabis rent

Conventional users on this corridor pay $20–24/SF NNN for a freestanding drive-thru box. The tenant pays $30. You are being asked to capitalize the premium at the same rate as the base rent.

3. The credit is two individuals

A single-store operator in a state where wholesale flower prices fell 23–26% year over year and 300+ dispensaries now compete. Personal guarantees are only as good as the balance sheets behind them — which the OM does not show.

04

Best tenant types, ranked

Re-tenanting strategy

A 4,400 SF freestanding building with a vault, a 3-lane drive-thru and three acres is a specific instrument. It rewards some users and punishes others.

Tier 1 — pursue first

Credit unions & community banks

The single cleanest backfill: the building already is one. Vault, drive-thru lanes, teller infrastructure and parking transfer at near-zero TI. Branch counts are in national decline, but South Jersey credit unions continue to add suburban locations. Rent $22–26/SF, fast occupancy, investment-grade-adjacent credit.

Urgent care, dental, veterinary, dialysis

Deepest tenant pool in the market, and the format works: drive-thru bay converts to a covered patient drop-off, the parking field is oversized, and Route 30 visibility drives walk-in volume. Costs $60–100/SF to fit out, but these tenants sign 10–15 year NNN leases with bumps — which is what makes a future sale financeable.

Tier 2 — highest rent, highest friction

Another NJ cannabis licensee

The only use that holds $30/SF. But Waterford Township caps retail cannabis at two licenses and the incumbent holds one — so the demand pool is a single seat, and any replacement needs CRC approval at this specific premises plus local sign-off. Scarcity supports the rent; scarcity also means there may be no bidder. Do not underwrite this as the base case.

QSR / coffee drive-thru

The 3-lane drive-thru is genuinely valuable — but 4,400 SF is nearly double a modern QSR prototype. The realistic execution is a demise to ~2,200 SF or a pad rebuild, not a straight re-let. Rent per foot is the best in the market ($38–48), total rent is not, and capex is $100–175/SF. Best pursued on the excess land as a ground lease, leaving the existing box to a Tier 1 user.

Tier 3 — the floor, and the option nobody priced

Conventional retail & service

Liquor, convenience, pharmacy, auto parts, tax and check-cashing, fitness. Always available at $18–22/SF. Treat this as the downside case, not a plan.

The excess land — ±2.5 unused acres

At ~3% coverage this site is mostly empty. In PHB zoning a subdivided highway pad could support a QSR, car wash or medical pad at $50,000–$90,000 per year of ground rent — potentially adding more income than the building itself. This is unpriced in the offering and unverified. It is also the one item that could justify paying up. Confirm Pinelands Commission jurisdiction, sewer/septic capacity and subdivision feasibility before assigning it a dollar.

The real estate is genuinely good — corner position on a 15,370-VPD corridor, a rare 3-lane drive-thru, three acres in a supply-constrained township, and a basis below replacement cost. The problem is not the dirt. It is that the offering asks you to pay a market cap rate for an above-market rent on a below-market credit with no escalations.

Buy it as a value-add re-tenanting play with a 6.5-year income runway, not as a passive net lease bond. Price it so that the conventional re-let outcome still clears your cost of capital, and treat renewal and the excess land as upside you did not pay for.

Ways to bridge the gap without moving price

Buy the escalations

Ask the seller to negotiate 2–3% annual bumps into the remaining term before closing. At 2.5% a year the lease is worth roughly $90,000 more in present value and materially more at exit — often easier for a seller to deliver than a price cut.

Seller-funded rollover reserve

Hold back $200,000–$250,000 at closing against 2033 TI and leasing commissions, releasable to the seller if the tenant renews. This prices the exact risk in dispute instead of arguing about cap rates.

Seller financing

Given that institutional debt is effectively unavailable for cannabis-tenanted assets, a seller note at 6.5–7.5% converts negative leverage into positive and can support a higher headline price.

Extend, don't just renew

If the tenant's sales support it, negotiate a five-year extension now in exchange for a modest near-term concession. Ten years of term instead of 6.5 changes the buyer pool at exit and is worth 75–100 bps of cap rate.

Bottom line for the buyer. He can re-rent this building for $88,000–$106,000 to a conventional tenant, or hold the $132,000 only by finding another licensed cannabis operator in a township that permits two. The best backfills, in order, are a credit union or community bank (turnkey — the vault and drive-thru are already there), then urgent care or veterinary (deepest demand, highest TI), then a QSR pad on the excess land while a Tier 1 user takes the box.

At $1,600,000 the expected unlevered return is 4.3%. At $1,325,000 it is 8.1%, with the renewal case at 11.9% and a downside that still clears zero. That is where this deal works.
